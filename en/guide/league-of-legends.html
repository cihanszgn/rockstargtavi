What League of Legends is and where it stands in 2026

#

In League of Legends two teams of five meet on a map called Summoner's Rift. Each player picks a champion. The goal is to destroy the Nexus in the enemy base. Along the way there are turrets, monsters and side objectives.

The game moves in patches. In November 2026 the current patch is 26.22 (Riot's official patch notes). It covers topics like Mel's midscope, the ARAM Mayhem event and some champions returning to Classic mode. The next patch is reported as 26.23 (according to community sources).

The 2026 ranked year is split into three seasons: Season 1 (January 8 to April 28), Season 2 (April 29 to July 28) and Season 3 (started July 29, 2026). The annual reset is expected in early January; check the official announcement for the exact date.

Tip

Numbers in this guide are accurate as of November 2026. Patches can change small details; check Riot's support pages for exact rules.

Before you start: account, install, system

#

League of Legends is free and played with a Riot account. According to Riot's official support page, the minimum system is an Intel i3-530 or AMD FX-4100 processor, a GeForce 400 series or Radeon HD 6570 or Intel HD 4600 graphics card (1 GB of video memory), 2 GB of RAM, 16 GB of disk space and Windows 10 (build 19041 or higher) or Windows 11. Windows 11 needs TPM 2.0. Recommended: Intel i5-8250 or Ryzen 3 1200, GeForce 560, 4 GB of RAM and an SSD.

  • On macOS only Macs with Intel processors are supported.
  • On Windows 11 TPM 2.0 must be on; you may need to enable it in the BIOS.
  • After install the game downloads patches automatically; allow time on first launch.
  • Choose your region (server) during install; changing it later is hard.

Watch out

Download the game only from Riot's official site. Links on other sites promising 'free RP' or accounts can get your account stolen.

Your first matches step by step

#
  1. 1
    Try the tutorial and games against AI

    Learn movement, attacks and abilities in the tutorial and Co-op vs. AI matches.

  2. 2
    Swiftplay and ARAM at level 3

    At account level 3 the Summoner's Rift Swiftplay mode and ARAM open; get used to it with short matches.

  3. 3
    Practice Tool at level 9

    Use the Practice Tool to try champions and items on your own.

  4. 4
    Normal Draft at level 10

    Normal Draft is a 5v5 Summoner's Rift match with a pick and ban phase.

  5. 5
    Ranked at level 30

    Ranked (Solo/Duo and Flex) needs account level 30, at least 20 champions and 10 Swiftplay or Normal Draft games.

Choosing a champion and role for the first weeks

#

Summoner's Rift has five roles: Top, Jungle, Mid, Bot and Support. Classic champions are unlocked through Classic Level, the store or the weekly free rotation. The table below shows typical choices that have stayed simple for a long time; they are common beginner picks, not an official Riot recommendation, and balance may change with patches.

RoleSimple champion exampleWhy it is easy
TopGarenDurable; what to do is clear
MidAnnieSimple abilities; can start fights
BotAsheDeals damage from range; has a slow
SupportSorakaHeals; helps the team right away
JungleAmumuStarts fights easily; sturdy

Tip

For a week, play the same role with the same two champions. Learning one well moves you forward faster than trying many.

According to Riot's official dev post, runes and masteries were simplified in Classic mode and IP was brought back, so you can save currency by playing to unlock champions. The key point for beginners is not to drown in rune and item details in the first days: use the game's recommended presets and focus first on surviving in lane.

RoleMain jobYour first goal
TopLane alone; tankiness and fightingLast-hit minions, do not die
JungleKills camps, helps the lanesLearn one safe route, watch dragon and Herald
MidThe middle of the map; close to every sideDeal damage, give the jungler information
BotSteady ranged damageFarm without dying with your support
SupportVision, protection and team helpPlace wards, keep bot lane safe

If you do not know which to choose, start with Mid or Support: Mid sits in the middle of the map so you see the flow of the game; Support adds value to the team without demanding much mechanical skill. In champion select the player who calls a role first gets it; if you are last you may have to play what is left, so learn 2 roles and 2 champions in each.

Core mechanics: lane, minions, objectives

#

The first part of a match is the lane phase: killing minions gives gold. You earn gold by getting the last hit, so last-hitting minions is a basic habit.

  • Turrets: Outer turrets fall first, then inner turrets, then the Nexus.
  • Monsters: Objectives like the Dragon, Baron and Rift Herald give the team lasting power.
  • Map awareness: Check the minimap every few seconds; an enemy leaving their lane is a danger sign.
  • Team play: Do not chase alone; a lost turret or dragon does not come back.

The most lasting principle for new players: not dying earns you more gold than dying. Every death costs you time and gold.

TermMeaning
CS (creep score)The number of minions you kill; key for gold and levels
Last hitLanding the final blow on a minion to take its gold
GankThe jungler or another role surprising a lane to kill the opponent
WardAn item that provides vision on the map

TiltGetting angry after a bad run and making bad decisions

Minion waves, gold and vision

#

Minions spawn in waves at set intervals and walk down the lane. Managing the wave well is how you gain an advantage without fighting: if you push the wave into the enemy tower, the enemy stands badly near their tower; if it comes toward you, you farm safely and are protected from the jungler. Caster and cannon minions give more gold; try to kill those first.

TopicWhat to doNote
Last hit (CS)Hit the minion when its health is low; try the same speed and timing every timeRiot has not officially stated a CS per minute target; first measure your own average, then raise it a step each week
GoldKills, towers and objectives also give gold; but you lose gold when you dieDo not idle: going back to buy an item raises your fighting power
RecallGo back when you can afford a new item and the lane is safeIf you leave while the wave is near your tower you lose little
GankGuess which side the enemy jungler is onIf the jungle is unknown, do not step forward

Vision is half of a fight. A Stealth Ward (a free, timed trinket) gives you view on the map; a Control Ward is bought and reveals invisible enemies and traps. Ward duration and price may change with patches; read the shop text. Placing a ward around objectives such as the dragon, the Baron and the river entrance lets you know in advance that the enemy is coming.

  • Place a ward on every recall; an empty ward slot is a wasted chance.
  • If the enemy jungler's place is unknown, do not chase objectives.
  • Before going into dark areas such as brush and entrances, look at the minimap.
  • Even if you are not Support, buy a Control Ward; it is one of the most useful purchases for the team (check the price in-game).

Tip

In your first week, let your goal not be a kill count but to lower your death count from the previous match.

Practical rule: 30-60 seconds before an objective, push the wave in your lane, go back, buy a ward and approach with your team. Before fighting for an objective count where the enemy jungler is and whether your teammates are alive.

Watch out

The times above change with patches. The numbers rest on community sources; if a Riot patch note says otherwise, follow it.

Runes and item logic

#

Runes are lasting boosts you pick before the match starts. There are five paths: Precision, Domination, Sorcery, Resolve and Inspiration. You choose one keystone and the smaller runes tied to it. Do not drown in detail: the game offers ready recommended rune pages for your champion; use them for the first weeks and do not change them.

QuestionSimple answer
Which item should I buy?Start from the shop's recommended items tab; it offers a ready path for your champion
Damage or durability?If your team lacks damage buy damage; if you always die buy durability
What if the enemy has many mages?Look for items with magic resistance; decide at your first recall
When should I buy?On every recall buy the best item your gold allows; do not enter a fight with unspent gold
  • Do not keep changing runes and items in one patch; play the same setup for at least 5 matches to see the difference.
  • Read what your keystone does: some work on the first hit, some in short fights.
  • Do not put a durability item on a mage or an attack speed item on an ability champion without thinking; read your own champion's ability text.

Tip

Understanding why an item is recommended makes you improve faster than memorizing the list.

Settings: starting values

#

These are not official values; they are commonly suggested starting points. Try them in the Practice Tool.

SettingStarting suggestion
Mouse sensitivityMedium; a value that lets you pan the map comfortably
Attack moveRight-click to move and A to attack; try both
Ability keysTry Quick Cast (Q, W, E, R fire right away instead of at the cursor)
MinimapKeep it bottom right; look at it often
AudioKeep effect volume on to hear enemy abilities

Tip

When you change a setting, play a few matches with the same value; do not change your mind right away.

In the launcher check your language and region choice before installing; Riot publishes the region list on its support page and the Turkey region is there. During a match learn the hotkeys one at a time: first Q, W, E, R for abilities, then the item keys. Watch three things in a match: your own health, the cooldown of the enemy's ability and the minimap. Making a habit of checking them every few seconds is the most valuable practice of your first week.

Ranked system and rank table

#

The ranked tiers are: Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Emerald, Diamond, Master, Grandmaster, Challenger. From Iron to Diamond each tier splits into divisions IV to I and you progress with LP (League Points). Master and above form a single leaderboard.

In your first 5 placement matches you do not lose LP; the starting rank can be at most Diamond III. For first-time players Swiftplay and Normal Draft MMR also count. Leaving a placement match as AFK counts as a loss.

Common beginner mistakes

#
  • Jumping into ranked unprepared; get used to it with Normal Draft first.
  • Not last-hitting minions and constantly looking for fights.
  • Not looking at the minimap.
  • Blaming teammates; when you die, look for the mistake in yourself.
  • Trying a different champion every match.
  • Playing back-to-back matches while tilted; take a break.
  1. 1
    Every day

    Practice last-hitting for 10 minutes in the Practice Tool.

  2. 2
    After each match

    Find the reason for one of your deaths and fix it in the next match.

  3. 3
    Every week

    Play at least five matches with the same two champions.

  4. 4
    Every month

    Review your settings, but do not change too much.

When you make a mistake do not blame yourself; pick one mistake, find its cause and fix only that in the next match. Progress comes from small, steady fixes.

Account safety and behavior rules

#

Your account is your digital vault. Turn on two-step verification and do not share your password with other sites.

Watch out

Cheat software, scripts, automation bots, rank boosting services, buying or selling accounts, skin gambling or betting sites and pirate or fake 'free RP' sites can lead to a permanent ban. Stay away from them.

  • Going AFK or deliberately ruining a match (feeding) brings penalties.
  • Be respectful to teammates; report and mute toxic chat.
  • Playing on a second account against lower-ranked players (smurfing) is unfair; do not do it.
  • Never share your account with anyone.

Frequently asked questions

#
Is League of Legends free?

Yes. The game is free; purchases are for cosmetics.

When can I play ranked?

After account level 30, at least 20 champions and 10 Swiftplay or Normal Draft games.

How is my first rank set?

With 5 placement matches; the start is at most Diamond III.

Can my rank drop?

Yes, demotion and rank decay exist.

Which champion should I start with?

Pick a simple, durable champion; play the same two for a week.

Can I play on a phone?

No; this guide is for Windows PC.

What is the difference between Normal Draft and Swiftplay?

Swiftplay is shorter and faster and opens at account level 3. Normal Draft opens at level 10 and has a pick and ban phase; it is closer to the ranked experience.
