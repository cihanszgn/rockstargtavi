What Fortnite is and where it stands in 2026

#

Fortnite is Epic Games' free-to-play title. It started as a battle royale (last one standing wins), but today it is less a single game than a platform: different modes, community-made islands and separate experiences such as music and racing all sit under one account. This guide covers the most-played core experience, Battle Royale.

In November 2026 the game is in the Chapter 7 "Treasure Hunters" mini-season. According to Epic's announcement it began on 1 November with update v43.00, bringing the new Crossbones Isle area, a One Piece collaboration and captains who change weekly. Epic confirmed the Chapter 7 end event for Saturday, 5 December 2026; what follows had not been officially announced on the day this guide was written.

Tip

Fortnite changes the map, items and rules every season. In v43.00, for example, gold bars and vending machines were removed. That is why this guide teaches lasting habits rather than weapon lists.

Before you start: account, install, requirements

#

You need a free Epic Games account to play. If you play on PC, Epic's official help page gives the following hardware.

  • Minimum: Intel HD 4000 or Radeon Vega 8 graphics, Core i3-3225 processor, 64-bit Windows 10 22H2.
  • Recommended: a GeForce GTX 960 or Radeon R9 280 class DirectX 11 graphics card (2 GB video memory), Core i5-7300U or Ryzen 3 3300U, 16 GB RAM, NVMe SSD, 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.
  • For the Epic preset: an RTX 3070 or RX 6700 XT class card (8 GB video memory) and a Core i7-8700 or Ryzen 7 3700X.
  • Install size: Epic's requirements page and store page do not give a disk size; it is not officially stated. Check the size shown in the launcher before installing.
  • Turkish: Epic's store page lists Turkish among text languages; there is no audio language (N/A).
  • Crossplay: Epic counts Fortnite as a game that supports crossplay across PC, PlayStation (PS4, PS5), Xbox (Series S and X), Nintendo (Switch and Switch 2) and mobile. For cross-progression, logging in with the same Epic account is the basis; account-linking details are not officially announced.

Tip

Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) on day one; the real gain is not losing your account.

Your first matches, step by step

#
  1. 1
    Start with a no-building mode

    In Zero Build you do not need to put up walls and ramps. It is the gentlest entry for learning aiming, movement and the storm.

  2. 2
    Choose where to land

    When jumping from the Battle Bus pick a spot away from the crowds. The first goal is to loot without dying.

  3. 3
    Collect a weapon, shield and healing

    Get at least two weapons and a healing item. Items of a rarer color are generally stronger; being able to fight with what you hold is enough.

  4. 4
    Stay ahead of the storm

    Check the map often and head toward the safe circle early. Staying in the storm deals damage and exposes you.

  5. 5
    Take one lesson from each match

    When you die ask one question: did I have to take that fight? Fixing one thing per match is enough.

Tip

Winning is not the goal in your first matches. Surviving the first ten minutes and getting close to the top three is already a good start for most newcomers.

#

Below are the main modes seen on Epic's official pages. When a mode is available can vary by season; the in-game menu is always the right list.

Tip

If you are just starting, play Zero Build for three or four days, then try the building mode. Both count toward the same Battle Pass: according to Epic, Zero Build and Battle Royale share XP progression.

Core mechanics

#

Three things always matter in Fortnite: movement, resources and position. Even when the meta items change, this trio stays constant.

  • Movement: running, sliding and climbing are not only for getting around but for leaving enemy fire. A player running in a straight line at constant speed is an easy target.
  • Resources: in the building mode, gathering materials (wood, stone, metal) is part of being fight-ready. Zero Build drops this job; healing and shields take its place.
  • Position: high ground, cover and a spot near the edge of the circle give an advantage. A player left in the open is often shot from several sides.
  • Health and shield: two separate bars. Topping up your shield before a fight beats trying to heal in the middle of one.
  • Sound: gunfire, footsteps and chests give information. Playing with headphones, and enabling the visual sound indicator if you need it, helps.

Tip

After every fight find a safe spot to loot and heal. A third player may be waiting while you collect items.

Building and editing basics

#

Building exists only in the modes that allow it (not in Zero Build). The logic is simple: there are four piece types and all of them spend materials. The piece and material names in this section can change by season, and a quiet practice island is the calmest place to try them.

PieceWhat it is for
WallCover from fire, quick shelter during a fight.
FloorCovering the ground under you and blocking damage from above.
RampGaining height; high ground is an advantage.
RoofClosing the top and forming an enclosed room.
  1. 1
    Place a single piece first

    In an open area place a wall, a floor and a ramp in turn. Look at where each piece lands, not at speed.

  2. 2
    Combine ramp and wall

    A ramp with a wall in front gives both height and cover. In a fight it is the first useful structure.

  3. 3
    Learn editing

    Editing lets you open a door or a window in a piece. It creates passages without rebuilding the structure. Do it slowly first, then speed up.

  4. 4
    Protect your materials

    Every piece spends materials. Building pointless towers leaves you with none in the middle of a fight.

Tip

There is no rush: ten minutes of building practice a day teaches more than hours of trying it in fights.

Weapon rarity and item choice

#

According to Epic's developer documentation, weapons are ranked by rarity, and rarity affects damage, magazine size, spread and recoil. Rarity is shown by the label and by the number of stars.

RarityStars (Epic docs)Usual color
Common1Gray
Uncommon2Green
Rare3Blue
Epic4Purple
Legendary5Gold/orange
Mythic6Golden yellow

The star count and order come from Epic's document; the color column is general game knowledge. Shades can vary with the season's interface, so the stars and the label are what you can rely on.

  • Rarity is not magic: a rarer weapon of the same class is generally stronger; but if you hit well with what you hold, do not leave a safe place to chase rarity.
  • Range variety: carrying one close-range and one mid-to-long range weapon is the most dependable combination.
  • Ammo matters: each weapon uses its own ammo type; make sure you have spare ammo before entering a fight.
  • The list changes each season: Epic brings back or removes weapons each season (in v43.00 some weapons were unvaulted). Check the season's patch notes for the current pool.

Tip

Instead of memorizing weapon names, try each new weapon first in a practice area; feeling its damage and recoil is worth more than knowing the list.

Settings: solid starting values

#

Safe starting values. All of them need fine-tuning for you; change only one at a time.

  • Frame rate limit: equal to your monitor's refresh rate. A steady lower rate beats a fluctuating high one.
  • Graphics: start with a low or medium preset. Lower shadows and effects, and set view distance and texture quality according to your memory.
  • Sensitivity: start at a middle value and adjust in small steps. Control matters more than speed when aiming.
  • Controller dead zones (gamepad): raise them just enough to cut stick drift without adding noticeable delay.
  • Accessibility: colorblind options and the visual sound indicator live in the in-game settings; turn them on from day one if you need them.

Tip

After changing settings, test them somewhere calm, not in a fresh match. That way you judge them away from the pressure of a fight.

Progression: the Battle Pass and the season

#

Playing through the season earns you experience that unlocks Battle Pass rewards. The Battle Pass costs 800 V-Bucks and, since the March 2026 price change, gives 800 V-Bucks back; check the in-game Passes tab for the current price in your region.

During Treasure Hunters, Epic announced regular events such as Captain's Days (1, 8, 12, 15 and 22 November), Mastery Mondays and Treasure Hours on Saturdays. Fortnite OG runs its own separate season; you will find out which one suits you after a few matches.

ItemOfficial information
PlayingFree; "Free" on the store page with in-app purchases (November 2026).
Battle Pass800 V-Bucks; gives back 800 V-Bucks when completed (since the March 2026 price change).
V-Bucks packsSmallest pack 800 V-Bucks / $8.99; largest 12,500 V-Bucks / $89.99 (US prices).
Fortnite Crew$11.99 per month on the store page; the page carries a change note for after 5 December 2026, so read the current terms before buying.
Epic Rewards20% back on purchases made through Epic payment (Markets Insider report).

Quests and XP: according to Epic's store page, Zero Build shares XP progression and Quests with Battle Royale. So whichever mode you play, your Battle Pass fills. Season-specific goals such as Sprite Mastery unlock weekly under the Treasure tab (Epic patch notes). The ranked season's reward steps are listed as Gold, Platinum, Diamond, Elite and Unreal; the full table of lower tiers and point thresholds is not officially announced.

  • First look at the season's goals and start with the ones that are easy to progress.
  • Do not try to finish everything in a week; the season is long and the events are spread over time.
  • Do not buy the Battle Pass unless you are sure the rewards are something you want; progress closes when the season ends.

Tip

The Chapter 7 end event is on 5 December; time any purchase decision around it, and read the details of what follows in the official announcement.

Parental controls, step by step

#

Epic's safety page explains what parental controls do. The order below follows the settings on that page; menu names can change with versions, so compare with what you see on screen.

  1. 1
    Open the child's Epic account

    In the Epic account settings open the Parental Controls section and set a PIN. Do not give this PIN to the child.

  2. 2
    Set payment permissions

    Require the PIN for Epic payments and choose whether V-Bucks can be spent on paid random items. This option does not exist in some regions.

  3. 3
    Pick the age rating

    Every experience in Fortnite has its own rating. Experiences above the rating you set do not open without the PIN.

  4. 4
    Allow or block creative tools

    Access to Creative mode and Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN) can be restricted separately.

  5. 5
    Set the play time

    According to Epic's page you can pick daily time windows for Fortnite and UEFN and set a time limit inside that window.

Watch out

Payments made on a console depend on the console platform's own parental settings (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo, Steam); the Epic payment PIN covers only Epic payments. Also check the settings of the platform your child plays on.

Common beginner mistakes

#
  • Dropping straight into a hot zone. Jumping into a crowded spot means being eliminated before you find gear.
  • Missing the storm. Forgetting the circle while looting leads to rushing and damage.
  • Looting in the open. While searching chests and ground items, check your surroundings.
  • Underestimating opponents and taking pointless fights. Sometimes disengaging is smarter than winning.
  • Wanting to buy everything. Cosmetics do not make you stronger; set your budget in advance.
  • Playing late and losing focus. Mistakes rise when you are tired; short sessions teach more.

Tip

Set just one target per day: practice storm timing today, inventory management tomorrow. Too many goals means learning none of them.

Account security and spending warning

#

Fortnite is free, but cosmetics and the Battle Pass are bought with V-Bucks, which cost real money. Treat spending as an entertainment budget and set a limit in advance.

  • Turn on two-factor authentication in your Epic account and enter your password only on Epic's official login page.
  • Set up parental controls for children (see the separate section); a payment PIN, age rating and play time can be set.
  • Do not click links promising free V-Bucks or files that look like game hacks; they are used to steal accounts.
  • Before spending, check that the price is shown in your local currency and what exactly it buys.

Watch out

Stay away from cheat software, aimbots, macros or special input devices, buying or selling accounts, V-Bucks or items, and having someone boost your rank. These can get your account banned permanently and are often scams.

Frequently asked questions

#
Is Fortnite free?

Yes, playing is free. Optional purchases cover cosmetics and the Battle Pass.

Do I have to learn building?

No. Zero Build has no building; you can learn it later if you want.

How much should I spend on the Battle Pass?

It is not required. The pass costs 800 V-Bucks and has given 800 V-Bucks back since March 2026; consider it only if you will play enough during the season.

When does Chapter 7 end?

Epic announced the end event for Saturday, 5 December 2026. Wait for official announcements on what comes after.

Are free V-Bucks sites safe?

No. They are mostly account-theft or scam attempts. Get V-Bucks only from the official store.

How do I make it safe for my child?

Turn on parental controls in the Epic account, require approval for chat and spending, and do not leave a payment method saved.
