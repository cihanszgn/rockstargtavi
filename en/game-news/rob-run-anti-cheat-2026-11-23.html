ROB RUN explores hardware bans and stricter servers

The developer says over 200,000 accounts are blocked, and separate servers with stricter access are planned to launch first.

November 23, 2026Source: Steam
ROB RUN
Image: SteamNews

ROB RUN's developer announced on Steam on November 23, 2026 that a new wave of bans has just rolled out. The team says it has blocked more than 200,000 accounts so far. Because cheaters can quickly make new accounts in a free-to-play game, the developer says a standard ban is not enough and is exploring hardware bans.

To make anti-cheat its top priority, the studio is temporarily delaying development of some upcoming in-game mechanics. It is also discussing extra security measures with the Easy Anti-Cheat (EAC) team, building its own cheat-detection tools and expanding its in-house anti-cheat team. The first planned step is separate servers with stricter access. Depending on the server, entry will require either a minimum amount of jail time or a set total of in-game purchases, and these servers will be matchmade separately from standard ones.

For players, the aim is fewer encounters with cheaters who return on new accounts, and the developer expects the change to reduce ban evasion sharply. Anti-cheat improvements will continue on all servers, and major security updates will be announced. The post does not give dates for the restricted servers.
